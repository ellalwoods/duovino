# Track A: How wine works

## The idea: the soil *is* the syllabus

The six soil strata from the prototype become six **chapters**. Each one sits on top of the one before, just as each chapter builds on the last. Your root grows down through them unit by unit, so the Roots tab is both the map of the course and your progress through it.

```
TOPSOIL    1 · How wine works            → the language every later chapter uses
CLAY       2 · The vine & its place      → why the same grape tastes different in different places
LIMESTONE  3 · The winery                → what the winemaker adds or takes away
SCHIST     4 · The great grapes          → uses 1–3 to understand each variety in its homes
CHALK      5 · Reading the Old World     → uses 4 to decode regions, labels and law
GRANITE    6 · Thinking like a sommelier → puts it all together: quality, blind tasting, buying
```

The depth roughly runs from a **WSET 1 refresher** (Topsoil), through **WSET 2** (Clay to Chalk), into **WSET 3-style "explain why"** reasoning (Granite, and the deeper questions throughout).

## How every unit works

| Step | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The Pour** (lesson) | 5–7 swipeable cards with one idea each, under ~50 words. Some cards are interactive: *tap to reveal*, *scales*, *side-by-side compares*. | You learn it before you're tested on it. |
| **2. The Taste** (practice) | 6–7 questions that test **only what the lesson taught**, in mixed formats: sort into groups, place on a scale, put in order, decode a label, blind deduction, and some multiple choice. | Varied, active recall rather than recognising the right option. |
| **3. From your cellar** | One recall question from an **earlier** unit is mixed in. | Interleaving: old knowledge keeps getting used. |
| **4. Lay down bottles** | Each concept becomes a cellar bottle, reviewed on a spaced schedule (1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35 → 90 days). | Long-term memory. |
| **5. Homework glass** *(later)* | A real-world mission, e.g. "Buy a Muscadet and a Napa Chardonnay and compare their body." | Turns screen knowledge into tasting skill. |

Each chapter ends with a **Checkpoint Tasting**: a longer mixed test with a blind-deduction finale. Passing it breaks through into the next stratum. Since you've done WSET 1, you'll be able to **test out** of Topsoil by taking its checkpoint first.

## The full syllabus (37 units + 6 checkpoints)

### 1 · TOPSOIL: How wine works
1. **What is wine?** Fermentation, inside a grape, why red wine is red, ripeness, dry vs sweet ✅ *built*
2. **The structure of wine:** sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body, balance ✅ *built*
3. **Tasting like a pro:** look → smell → sip → think, colour and age, the three layers of aroma, finish, BLIC ✅ *built*
4. **The styles of wine:** light vs full whites and reds, rosé, sparkling, sweet, fortified
5. **Wine & food:** what food does to wine (sweet, umami, acid, salt, chilli, fat)
6. **Serving & storing:** temperatures, glasses, decanting, opening sparkling, keeping bottles
- ✦ *Checkpoint: The Topsoil Tasting*

### 2 · CLAY: The vine & its place
1. **A vine's year:** anatomy and the growing cycle, budburst to dormancy
2. **Climate:** cool vs warm, continental vs maritime vs Mediterranean
3. **Terroir:** latitude, altitude, slope and aspect, water bodies, soil
4. **In the vineyard:** yields, pruning, canopy, harvest timing
5. **Threats & friends:** frost, hail, drought, rot, and the *noble* rot
- ✦ *Checkpoint*

### 3 · LIMESTONE: The winery
1. **Making white wine**
2. **Making red & rosé**
3. **The spice rack:** oak, lees, malolactic conversion
4. **Making it sweet:** stopping fermentation, dried grapes, noble rot, ice
5. **Making it sparkle:** traditional method vs tank method
6. **Making it stronger:** Sherry & Port
7. **Time in the bottle:** what ageing does, and which wines deserve it
- ✦ *Checkpoint*

### 4 · SCHIST: The great grapes
*Each unit = one grape + 2–3 contrasting homes, applying Clay and Limestone.*
1. **Chardonnay:** Chablis → Côte de Beaune → Napa
2. **Sauvignon Blanc:** Sancerre → Marlborough → Bordeaux Blanc
3. **Riesling:** Mosel → Alsace → Clare Valley
4. **Pinot Noir:** Burgundy → Central Otago → Sonoma
5. **Cabernet Sauvignon & Merlot:** Left Bank, Right Bank, Napa
6. **Syrah / Shiraz:** Northern Rhône → Barossa
7. **The supporting cast:** Pinot Grigio, Chenin Blanc, Grenache, Malbec, Tempranillo…
- ✦ *Checkpoint*

### 5 · CHALK: Reading the Old World
1. **How wine law works:** PDO/PGI, AOC/DOC/DO, the quality pyramid
2. **France beyond the big names:** Loire, Rhône, Alsace, Beaujolais
3. **Champagne**
4. **Italy:** Piedmont, Tuscany, Veneto
5. **Spain:** Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Sherry country
6. **Germany:** the Prädikat ladder
7. **Portugal:** Douro, Port, Vinho Verde
- ✦ *Checkpoint*

### 6 · GRANITE: Thinking like a sommelier
1. **Judging quality:** BLIC in practice
2. **Deductive blind tasting:** from clues to conclusion
3. **Faults:** corked, oxidised, reduced, Brett
4. **Vintage variation:** why the year matters (and when it doesn't)
5. **Wine lists & buying smart**
- ✦ *Final checkpoint: The Grand Cru Tasting*

## What happens to the original four flights
They become **Tasting Specials** on the Today tab: optional bonus flights, with the seasonal one highlighted. Their content will later be folded into the units they belong to (Winter → Clay 1 and 4, Cool vs Warm → Clay 2 and Schist 1, Loire → Chalk 2, Barolo → Chalk 4).

# Track B: Reading the label

Every Track B lesson **starts from a label** and works outwards: label → appellation → grapes → rules → taste. On the Wine List tab it's laid out like a restaurant wine list, with countries as sections, regions as headings and each appellation unit as an entry.

**Unlocking:** Track B requires **Topsoil** (Track A, chapter 1) first, so tasting vocabulary like "high acidity" and "full body" is already familiar. The gate counts only the Topsoil units that are written (currently 1.1–1.3), so it grows as 1.4–1.6 are added. After that, the three B0 units come in order. Finishing B0 opens every region, and units within a region unlock in order.

## B0. How labels work ✅ *built*

| Unit | Covers |
|---|---|
| **B0.1 Place, not grape** | European labels name the place, so the appellation tells you the grapes. Where the appellation sits on French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese and German labels. Exceptions: Alsace, IGP Pays d'Oc, most of Germany and Austria |
| **B0.2 The quality ladders** | EU PDO/PGI. France: AOC/AOP, IGP, Vin de France. Italy: DOCG, DOC, IGT. Spain: DOCa/DOQ, DO, Vino de Pago. Germany: Prädikatswein, Qualitätswein, Landwein (a Prädikat means ripeness, not sweetness) |
| **B0.3 Law words & sales words** | Grand Cru, Premier Cru, Cru Classé, Classico, Superiore, Riserva/Reserva, plus unregulated terms such as Vieilles Vignes and Réserve |

B0 deliberately gives **no** appellation-specific numbers (ageing minimums, grape percentages). Those belong in the appellation units, after the rules have been checked.

## Rule-checking before a unit is written

Appellation rules (permitted grapes, minimum percentages, ageing minimums, sweetness terms) change from time to time, and the app's credibility depends on getting them right. So:

1. **Source first.** Before writing an appellation unit, check every rule it states against the official text:
   - France: the appellation's *cahier des charges* (INAO)
   - Italy: the *disciplinare di produzione*
   - Spain: the *pliego de condiciones*
   - Portugal, Germany, Austria and Hungary: the equivalent official specification
   - New World: the national labelling law (e.g. the US TTB for AVAs and percentage rules, Wine Australia for GIs)
2. **Record it in the data.** Each unit gets `sources` (official document and version/date) and `checked` (the date of the check). A unit gets `lesson` and `questions` only once both are filled in. Until then it shows as *"Still being poured"* on the Wine List.
3. **Re-check on a schedule,** e.g. yearly, or whenever a region announces changes.

**Content already in the app that needs the same check:** the original Tasting specials state some rules: Barolo 38 months and Riserva 62 months, the four Chablis tiers, Icewine picking temperatures. They should be checked against their disciplinare / cahier des charges too, or retired once Track B covers them.

**What the official texts changed or confirmed in the spec:**

| Item | Spec | Official text | Change made |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grand Cru villages | 17 | 17, named in Ch. I §II b | none |
| Premier Cru villages | 42 | 42, named in Ch. I §II c | none |
| Permitted grapes | 7 | **8 main varieties: adds Chardonnay rose (2025)**, plus Voltis | lesson 3 updated |
| Voltis | n/a | ≤5% of an estate's AOC vineyard; ≤10% of a blend; never alone; needs a signed agreement | added to lesson 3 |
| "Minor" grapes | Arbane, Petit Meslier, Pinot Blanc, Pinot Gris | Legally all are **main varieties** ("cépages principaux") | worded as "rarer", not "minor" |
| Hand-picking | required | Rule is "whole bunches only" (any method that can't do that is banned), which in effect means hand-picking | reworded |
| Pressing | cuvée vs taille | Max **102 L per 160 kg**; fractioning "per Champagne custom", **no figures** for cuvée/taille | no cuvée/taille volumes stated |
| Tirage | n/a | Not before 1 January after harvest | added |
| Lees ageing | 15 months NV, 3 years vintage | Sale ≥15 months from tirage (vintage ≥36 months); **no disgorgement before 12 months** | both stated |
| Dosage bands | as listed | Match Reg. 2019/33 Annex III A; Brut Nature also requires no sugar added after second fermentation | none |
| Blanc de Blancs | 100% Chardonnay | "White grapes only" (no labelling rule of its own) | "white grapes only, in practice almost always Chardonnay" |
| Vintage | n/a | 100% that year's grapes; max 80% of a year's wine may be sold as vintage; vintage also on the cork | added to lessons 1 and 6 |
| Grape on label | n/a | Only if 100% that grape; small type | added to lesson 6 |
| Village on label | n/a | 100% from that village; non-cru villages need "Vigne de" / "Vignoble de" | added to lesson 2 |
| "AOC" on label | may omit | Reg. 2019/33 Art. 23 allows omitting "protected designation of origin" for Champagne | source recorded |
| Producer codes | NM, RM… | Cahier des charges requires the CIVC registration code (XII h); **code meanings are set by the CIVC**, not in the official texts read | noted on lessons 1 and 7 |
| Coteaux Champenois | still red & white | Still **white, rosé and red** | lesson 9 fixed |
| Rosé des Riceys | Pinot Noir | Still rosé, 100% Pinot Noir, commune of Les Riceys only | none |
