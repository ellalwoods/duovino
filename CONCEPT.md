# Duovino — Concept v0.1

> *Working title.* A daily wine-learning app that feels less like a quiz and more like a cellar you build, a vineyard you tend, and a table you sit at.

---

## 1. The one-line pitch

**Learning wine should feel like wine: alive, seasonal, sensory, and better with time.**

Most learning apps look the same: a cartoon mascot, a winding path of bubbles, a guilt-trip streak counter. Duovino keeps the good part (five minutes a day, small wins) but swaps the rest for a world built from wine itself: its seasons, its soil, its cellars, its labels, the stain a glass leaves on a tablecloth.

## 2. Who it's for

- You've done **WSET Level 1** (or you just love wine) and want to go deeper, roughly **WSET 2 → 3** depth, without it feeling like revision.
- You drink real wine. The app should make every real glass a lesson, not just the screen time.
- You'll only keep using it if it's **beautiful and fun**, so design isn't decoration, it's the retention plan.

---

## 3. The big idea: the Vine, the Soil, the Cellar

The home screen isn't a path. It's a single **vertical cross-section** of a vineyard, from sky down to cellar. You scroll down through it.

```
   ☀  SKY ............ the season & today's weather (your daily activity)
   🌿 THE VINE ....... what you're learning now (today's flight)
   ▒▒ THE SOIL ....... your mastery, shown as roots growing down through strata
   🍾 THE CELLAR ..... everything you've learned, stored as bottles that age
```

### ☀ The Sky — the season
The app runs on the **real vineyard calendar**. It's November now, so the vines are bare and it's pruning season: the Sky is grey and frosty and the featured content is about winter work, dormancy and why pruning sets the yield. In September, everything goes gold and **Harvest** becomes a live event with special challenges. You can switch between Northern and Southern hemisphere, which is a lesson in itself.

Your daily activity becomes the **weather**. Learn today and the sun comes out. Miss a day and you get cloud, then a bit of frost. No guilt-trip "streak lost!" screen. Each week rolls up into a **Vintage rating**: a great week is a great vintage, a patchy week is a "difficult year" (which, as every wine lover knows, can still make interesting wine).

### 🌿 The Vine — today's flight
Lessons come in **Flights**: 3–5 short, themed rounds (2–4 min each), the way a tasting flight works. Examples:
- *"The Loire in Three Glasses"* (Sancerre → Vouvray → Chinon)
- *"Why is Barolo so grippy?"* (tannin, Nebbiolo, ageing)
- *"Cool vs. Warm"* (the same grape grown in Chablis and the Napa Valley)

### ▒▒ The Soil — your roots
Vines that have to struggle send their roots deeper, and deeper roots make better wine. Same with you. **Hard questions you get right push your roots further down** through visible soil layers: topsoil, clay, limestone, schist, chalk, bedrock. How deep you've gone is your level. The soil types are real, and each new stratum unlocks a short "what this soil does to wine" note.

### 🍾 The Cellar — your knowledge, ageing
Every concept you learn (a grape, a region, a label term, a winemaking step) becomes a **bottle in your cellar**. This is how we make **spaced repetition** feel native to wine:

| Spaced-repetition state | In the cellar |
|---|---|
| Just learned | Fresh bottle, bright label |
| Due for review | Bottle is "ready to drink": it glows softly |
| Reviewed successfully | It **ages**: dust gathers, the label yellows, its vintage climbs |
| Long neglected | It gets **"corked"**: a gentle nudge to revisit, never a punishment |

Over months your cellar fills into a gorgeous, personal library: dusty Bordeaux racks, a corner of Rieslings, a single bottle of Tokaji you're weirdly proud of. **It's the trophy cabinet, the progress chart and the review queue all in one.**

## 4. The games

Every game trains a real wine skill. No filler quizzes.

### 🕵️ Blind
*A deduction game.* A mystery glass appears. Clues come one at a time, in proper tasting order: **appearance → nose → palate** ("pale lemon… high acidity… green apple, wet stone… light body, no oak"). Guess the grape or region as early as you dare. The fewer clues you need, the higher your score. It trains real sommelier-style reasoning and builds on the WSET tasting approach you already know.

### 🏷 Label Decoder
Fictional producers, realistic labels. Tap anything (*Spätlese, Gran Reserva, Premier Cru, Sur Lie, Brut Nature*) and pull the meaning out. Later rounds: "Which of these three labels is the dry one?"

### 🍷 The Blend
A tactile mixing game. Sliders pour grapes into a glass to hit a target: build a Left Bank Bordeaux, a GSM, a Champagne cuvée. The wine's colour, body and tannin change on screen as you pour. You learn *why* blends exist.

### 🧑‍🌾 Winemaker
A short decision sim with cause and effect, built for WSET 3-style "explain why" understanding. Pick when to harvest (sugar vs acidity), choose the press, ferment warm or cool, oak or steel, malolactic or not, then see the wine you made, scored and described. Do it again in a hot vintage. It turns the textbook's winemaking chapter into a toy.

### 🗺 Terroir Drop
A hand-drawn map with no names on it. Drop grapes and wines where they belong. Rivers, slopes and latitudes matter, so you learn *why* Riesling hugs the Mosel.

### 🍽 The Dinner Party
Guests arrive with dishes (salty, spicy, sweet, fatty, umami). Match a wine to each guest, and their faces tell you how it went. It teaches the actual pairing rules (sweetness, acidity, tannin vs. bitterness, chilli vs. alcohol), not "red with meat."

### 👃 Nose Quest *(the off-screen one)*
"Go and smell: a lemon, a green pepper, wet pavement after rain, a vanilla pod." Aroma memory is half of tasting, and no screen can teach it. Small real-world missions do.

## 5. Real Glass Mode — the reason to open it every week

The killer feature: **log the wine you're actually drinking.**

1. Snap or search the label.
2. A guided, beautifully designed tasting note (a playful version of the WSET systematic approach) walks you through appearance, nose, palate and conclusion.
3. The app links it to what you've learned ("This is a Rioja Crianza: you studied this in *Spain's Big Three*. Spot the American oak?").
4. It goes into your cellar as a **special bottle** with your own note, date and photo.

Your study and your actual drinking become one collection.

**Table Mode (social):** one person picks a bottle in secret, and the app turns it into a group *Blind* game for the dinner table. Phones pass round, everyone guesses, and the scores get a toast.

## 6. Design language

This is the part that has to feel unlike anything else.

### Colour: the glass is the palette
The UI palette is the **WSET colour spectrum** itself: lemon-green, lemon, gold, amber · pink, salmon, onion-skin · purple, ruby, garnet, tawny. The interface **tints to the wine you're studying.** In a Sancerre flight the screen glows pale lemon. A vintage Port flight turns it deep tawny. Your screen is the glass.

### Shape: rings, not bars
Progress isn't a bar, it's a **glass ring stain**, the mark a wine glass leaves on paper. A finished flight leaves a full, rich ring. A half-finished one leaves a faint, broken arc. Over time your profile page looks like a well-loved tasting notebook covered in rings.

### Texture & type: the label, not the app store
- **Paper, letterpress and engraving**, like old wine labels and vintage vineyard maps. Hand-drawn maps, not satellite views.
- A **classic serif** for headings (label-like), a clean **monospace** for tasting data, like a cellar inventory.
- Illustration in a consistent hand: etched lines, watercolour washes, the odd wine drip.

### Motion & touch: liquid
- **Swirl your phone** (gyroscope) to "open up" a glass and reveal a hint.
- **Wine legs** run slowly down the screen edge as a gentle timer.
- **Tilt to pour** in The Blend.
- Correct answers land with a soft **glass clink**. Finishing a flight pops a cork.

### Voice: the witty sommelier, not the nagging owl
No mascot begging you to come back. Personality lives in the copy: warm, knowing, a little cheeky. ("That's a Gewürztraminer. Lychee and rose, subtle as a perfume counter.") An optional character to explore: **Botty**, a speck of noble rot (*Botrytis*). Technically a fungus, makes the world's greatest sweet wines, and a great underdog.

## 7. Curriculum spine (draft)

Six tracks, woven together so each flight mixes them:

1. **Grapes**: the core ~20 international varieties, then the regional heroes
2. **Places**: France, Italy, Spain, Germany, then the New World
3. **Making**: vineyard → fermentation → ageing, sparkling and fortified methods
4. **Tasting**: structure, faults, quality assessment (BLIC: balance, length, intensity, complexity)
5. **Labels & Law**: classifications, quality tiers, the vocabulary
6. **Table**: food pairing, service, temperature, storage

Depth ramps from **"WSET 1 refresher" → "WSET 2 confident" → "WSET 3 curious."**

## 8. What makes it different (summary)

| Typical learning app | Duovino |
|---|---|
| Linear path of bubbles | Vertical vineyard: sky, vine, soil, cellar |
| Streak counter & guilt | Weather and weekly Vintages: kind, seasonal |
| XP bar | Roots growing through real soil types |
| Hidden review algorithm | A cellar where knowledge visibly *ages* |
| Generic quizzes | Deduction, blending, winemaking and pairing games |
| Screen-only | Real Glass Mode, Nose Quests, Table Mode |
| Flat brand colour | The UI is the colour of the wine in the glass |
| Progress bars | Glass ring stains |

## 9. Open questions to decide next

1. **Name.** "Duovino" leans on Duolingo's brand, which is fine as a working title but risky if this ever goes public. Ideas: *Lees* (wine "rests on the lees" to gain character), *Terroir*, *Rootstock*, *Decant*, *Cellar Door*, *Sur Lie*.
2. **Mascot or no mascot?** Witty-narrator-only vs. Botty the noble rot vs. something else.
3. **Platform first:** a mobile web app (fastest to build and try, works on your phone) vs. native iOS.
4. **First slice to prototype:** I'd suggest the **home cross-section + one complete Flight with the *Blind* game + the Cellar**. That proves the look, the core loop and the ageing mechanic in one go.
5. **How "real" should content be?** Real producers and labels, or fictional ones? Fictional avoids licensing issues and lets us design labels that teach.
